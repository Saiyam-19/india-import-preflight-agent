## Multi-Product Adversarial Round 2 — Not Approved

The reviewer accepted the catalog size, industry, admission isolation, seven-ticket order, and optional third-product result, but found two implementation-contract contradictions:

- packs were described as fully supported before the shared browser journey existed, while the selector required fully supported packs; and
- one synthetic PDF was incorrectly implied to verify extraction across every admitted product.

The correction introduces `candidate`, `source_admitted`, and `full_support`. Tickets 1 and 2 end at source admission and remain non-selectable/runtime-disabled. Ticket 3 uses a restricted harness and promotes each release-floor pack only after all three complete journeys pass. Extraction is verified for one synthetic router invoice only; its schema is reusable, all extracted facts require confirmation, and no other product extraction is claimed.

## Revision Submitted for Final Review

The revised plan adopts a provisional queue of Wi-Fi router, over-ear Bluetooth headphones, and indoor Wi-Fi/IP security camera. It preserves one shared journey and deterministic engine, sets a two-product release floor, targets three, defers any fourth product until release gates are green, and caps the architecture at 10 without claiming that capacity as coverage.

The implementation chain has seven sequential tickets: first pack admission; second pack admission; shared two-product journey; third product end-to-end; synthetic PDF extraction; Other-product fail-closed behavior; release proof. A blocked admission does not create a false selector entry or weaken the admission standard.
