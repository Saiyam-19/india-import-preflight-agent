# High-friction EXIM product families for the BWMI prototype

Research date: 23 August 2026

## Recommendation

The strongest problem wedge is **pre-flight due diligence for importing used or refurbished high-value medical equipment into India**. It is broader than one SKU but has a coherent user and workflow: an Indian authorised agent, medical-equipment importer, hospital, or diagnostic-chain operator assembling a legally complete import dossier before filing.

This wedge combines unusually dense documentary work across multiple authorities:

- CDSCO medical-device classification and the MD-14 application for an MD-15 import licence;
- MoEFCC permission and evidence under the Hazardous and Other Wastes regime for used/refurbished equipment;
- residual-life, serial-number, functionality, OEM-refurbishment, service-support, buyer and re-export evidence;
- CPCB E-Waste EPR where the equipment is covered;
- AERB eLORA approvals for equipment involving ionising radiation;
- DGFT import policy, IEC identity, ITC-HS classification, Customs documents and duty;
- possible BIS, WPC, packaging and labelling branches depending on the equipment.

The prototype should not submit applications or give legal clearance. It should assemble and cross-check a pre-flight evidence graph, explain the applicable routes, flag contradictions and missing evidence, generate regulator-specific dossier indexes, and simulate filing responses.

## Shortlist

|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Used/refurbished high-value medical equipment** | CDSCO, MoEFCC, CPCB, AERB for radiological products, DGFT, Customs | Very high | High if limited to pre-flight | Best differentiation and strongest multi-agency document story; requires explicit high-stakes disclaimer and refusal/escalation behavior. |
| **Connected telecom and IoT equipment** | TEC/MTCTE, WPC ETA, BIS, CPCB EPR, Legal Metrology, DGFT, Customs | High | Very high | Safest runner-up; larger electronics-market story and good branching across routers, gateways, access points and cameras. |
| **Lithium batteries and energy-storage products** | BIS, Battery Waste EPR, DGFT/Customs, transport evidence | High | High | Coherent and commercially relevant, but important dangerous-goods transport documents extend beyond Indian public-service portals. |
| **Solar PV cells/modules and project equipment** | MNRE ALMM, REE-IMS, BIS, DGFT/Customs, project-eligibility rules | High | High | Strong clean-energy narrative and rapidly changing list checks, but some ALMM questions concern project eligibility rather than import legality itself. |
| **Medical devices generally** | CDSCO/SUGAM, risk class A-D, QMS, plant/device master files, DGFT/Customs | Very high | Medium | Valuable but too broad unless restricted to one coherent family or new-vs-refurbished journey. |

## Why refurbished medical equipment is unusually agentic

### CDSCO dossier

CDSCO states that an authorised agent applies in **Form MD-14** through SUGAM for an **MD-15 import licence**. Its official checklist includes a covering letter, fee evidence, authenticated power of attorney, authorised-agent licence/registration, free-sale or marketing authorisations, overseas-site registration, audit reports, quality-management certificates, declaration of conformity, Plant Master File, Device Master File, labels/instructions, design and manufacturing information, clinical and post-market evidence, and other device-specific material.

Sources: [CDSCO medical-device portal](https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Medical-Device-Diagnostics/Medical-Device-Diagnostics/) and [MD-14/MD-15 checklist](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/medical-device/14MD.pdf).

### Used/refurbished equipment evidence

The MoEFCC checklist for used electrical/electronic equipment calls for Form 5, import justification, residual-life certification, serial numbers and functionality, OEM refurbishment and after-sales support for relevant capital equipment, State Pollution Control Board acknowledgement, re-export undertakings where applicable, and EPR registration where the equipment falls under the E-Waste Rules. For high-end, high-value used/refurbished medical devices, the official conditions specify at least seven years of residual life and additional equipment/buyer evidence.

Sources: [MoEFCC import/export checklist](https://www.moef.gov.in/uploads/2024/05/OM-Revised-Checklist-for-import-export-of-hazardous-and-other-wastes.pdf), [high-end high-value medical-equipment conditions](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/pdf/OM-Revised_list_of_High_End_High_Value_Used_Medical_Equipment.pdf), and [MoEFCC hazardous-waste import/export page](https://www.moef.gov.in/importexport-of-hazardous-other-waste).

### Radiological branch

AERB states that diagnostic X-ray equipment and facilities need statutory consents through eLORA. Manufacturer/supplier type or design approvals and end-user facility approvals can create additional branches for equipment using ionising radiation.

Sources: [AERB diagnostic radiology](https://www.aerb.gov.in/english/regulatory-facilities/radiation-facilities/application-in-medicine/diagnostic-radiology) and [AERB consenting process](https://aerb.gov.in/english/consenting-process-in-radiation-facilities).

## Runner-up: connected telecom and IoT equipment

Connected equipment is the best choice if the team wants lower legal and safety risk while remaining within the high-volume electronics sector.

- TEC says notified telecommunications equipment must satisfy MTCTE Essential Requirements and hold a valid conformity certificate before sale, deployment or use in India. The route includes product-specific ERs, test reports, model-family questions, authorised Indian representation and changing exemptions.
- WPC ETA is required for import, sale and use of many RF devices operating in de-licensed bands. Official guidance requires accredited RF reports and treats some classes, including drones and satellite equipment, differently.
- Depending on product attributes, BIS CRS/QCO, E-Waste EPR and retail-packaging requirements may also apply.

Sources: [TEC MTCTE](https://mtcte.tec.gov.in/aboutMTCTE), [TEC MTCTE publications](https://www.tec.gov.in/mandatory-testing-and-certification-of-telecom-equipments-mtcte), [WPC ETA service](https://eservices.dot.gov.in/taxonomy/term/307), [WPC ETA FAQ](https://eservices.dot.gov.in/sites/default/files/faqs/eta_faq.pdf), and [BIS Scheme II](https://www.bis.gov.in/product-certification/products-under-compulsory-certification/scheme-ii-registration-scheme/?lang=en).

## Other official constraints

- DGFT's Foreign Trade Policy treats several categories of second-hand goods as restricted and says notified second-hand electronics/IT goods may require authorisation; unregistered or non-compliant notified products are prohibited. [FTP 2023, paragraph 2.31](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/dgftprod/61d61bc2-272e-4880-b96c-c8f685a3b244/Foreign%20Trade%20Policy%202023.pdf)
- CPCB's Battery Waste Management procedure requires covered producers to register and provide product, sales, battery-material, document and fee information, with continuing EPR obligations. [CPCB battery EPR procedure](https://cpcb.nic.in/uploads/hwmd/Notice_for_BMW.pdf)
- MNRE maintains current ALMM lists for solar PV modules and cells, while REE-IMS monitors imported renewable-energy equipment. [MNRE ALMM](https://mnre.gov.in/en/approved-list-of-models-and-manufacturers-almm/) and [REE-IMS](https://reeims.mnre.gov.in/)
- DGFT's drone policy prohibits CBU/SKD/CKD imports except specified government, recognised R&D, drone-manufacturer R&D, defence and security cases; components are free. [DGFT Notification 54/2015-20](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/dgftprod/7d5fd1eb-ad39-4c99-b760-014223657469/Eng-Notification%2054%20dated%209%20Feb%202022%20ITC%28HS%29%202022%20_with%20Annexures.pdf)

## Prototype boundary

A credible five-day journey would support three synthetic cases:

1. A non-radiological refurbished device whose dossier is complete.
2. A radiological imaging device that triggers the AERB branch and contains inconsistent or expired evidence.
3. An ineligible or unsupported device that the agent refuses to mark ready and escalates to a specialist.

The agent should produce `ready for human filing`, `blocked`, or `specialist review required`, never `government approved`. All regulatory rules should be versioned and display their last-verification date. Live government submissions should remain mocked unless the hackathon provides an approved sandbox.
